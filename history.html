1951 – 2026 · Seventy-five years

Our history

Crossgar is an historic congregation — the first church of the Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster, born out of revival blessing and a stand for the gospel.

Crossgar Free Presbyterian Church is an historic congregation, being the very first congregation of the Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster. Since its constitution on 17th March 1951 it has stood foursquare for the glorious gospel of the Lord Jesus Christ. It is a church born out of revival blessing.

A gospel mission and a stand for the truth

Early in 1950, the committee of the Crossgar Mission Hall approached a 24-year-old evangelist, Rev. Ian R. K. Paisley, to ask whether he would conduct a gospel campaign in the town. After prayer, the date was fixed for February 1951. Feeling the Mission Hall would be too small, the committee — most of them office-bearers or members of Lissara Presbyterian Church — asked their Kirk Session for the use of the local Presbyterian church hall. This was unanimously granted.

However, the Down Presbytery, meeting on 8th January 1951, ruled that the mission should not go ahead in the Lissara church hall. Matters came to a head on the evening of Saturday 3rd February 1951, just ninety minutes before a march of witness to advertise the mission, when the Presbytery summoned the Lissara Session and demanded they reverse their decision. For two of the elders, Hugh James Adams and George K. Gibson, the high-handedness of the Presbytery was too much: they refused what they saw as an anti-gospel demand to cancel the campaign.

“Every seat was taken in the Killyleagh Street Church Hall, Crossgar, on Saturday afternoon, when the Rev. Ian R. K. Paisley conducted the opening service constituting the Crossgar congregation of the Free Presbyterian Church.” Church newsletter, March 1951

Born out of revival

The mission went ahead in the Killyleagh Street Mission Hall, and ninety-four souls professed faith in the Lord Jesus Christ. Out of that blessing, the opening service and constitution of Crossgar Free Presbyterian Church was held on 17th March 1951 — St Patrick’s Day. Five elders of Lissara were inducted, and that same year saw the formation of the Presbytery of the Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster.

From four churches to a worldwide witness

The formation of Crossgar was quickly followed by Rev. Ian Paisley’s congregation at Ravenhill and by the two North Antrim congregations of Cabra and Rasharkin. From four congregations in that first year, the growth of the new denomination continued until its witness had spread to every part of Northern Ireland — and, in time, to England, Scotland, the Republic of Ireland, North America, Australia, Nepal and beyond.

To one of the church’s beloved early elders, Sandy McAuley of Cabra, God gave the promise: “Though thy beginning was small, yet thy latter end should greatly increase.” From one small congregation in Crossgar, the Free Presbyterian Church became a worldwide communion, bound by one confession and one Lord.

The story in brief

  • January 1951

    The Down Presbytery bans a gospel mission from the Lissara church hall.

  • February 1951

    Elders are suspended; the mission proceeds in Killyleagh Street Mission Hall, where 94 souls profess faith in Christ.

  • 17 March 1951

    Crossgar Free Presbyterian Church is constituted — the first congregation of the new denomination.

  • 1951 onward

    Ravenhill, Cabra and Rasharkin join to form the Presbytery of the Free Presbyterian Church of Ulster.

  • 2026

    The congregation marks 75 years of gospel witness — giving thanks to God for His faithfulness.
